A good system design starts with requirements. Decide what must be correct, what must stay available, and how much traffic the system needs to handle. Then choose the components and trade-offs that meet those needs.

## Your learning path

Work through the course in order, or jump to a topic you need:

- **[Application architecture](/courses/system-design/docs/application-architecture/application-foundations):** Follow requests, explore scaling and caching, and understand production behavior.
- **[Networking](/courses/system-design/docs/networking/introduction):** Learn how IP, TCP, UDP, DNS, and HTTP move data between machines.
- **[API design](/courses/system-design/docs/api/introduction):** Compare REST, GraphQL, and RPC as contracts between services.

## Before you begin

You should be comfortable with basic programming and the idea of a client and server. You do not need experience building distributed systems.

## Make each lesson count

After each topic, explain what it does, when you would use it, and what you give up. Connect the answer to a small application you know. That habit turns a list of technologies into a reasoned design.
